# Disinformation in the Printing Press / Early Modern Era (~1450–1800)

## 1. Era Overview

Johannes Gutenberg's movable-type press (c. 1450) collapsed the cost of duplicating text and image. Within fifty years, presses operated in over 200 European cities, and a single workshop could turn out thousands of identical broadsides in days — a scale of message replication impossible in the manuscript age. Crucially, **printing was not centrally controlled**: while monarchies, the Papacy, and city councils all tried to license presses, the technology was cheap and portable enough that dissidents, exiles, and radicals could publish from places like Wittenberg, Geneva, Amsterdam, and (later) Philadelphia, beyond the reach of any one censor.

Literacy in 1500 was perhaps 5–10% across Europe but rose sharply in Protestant regions, reaching 30–50% in cities like Amsterdam and London by 1700. Even so, illiterates participated through **woodcut images** (the visual punch of a Cranach Pope-as-Antichrist), **read-alouds in taverns and markets**, and ballad-sheets. This created the first true mass-information environment: vernacular cheap print, emotionally charged imagery, anonymous authorship, and rapid reprint networks that could carry a rumor from Augsburg to London in weeks. Religious schism, dynastic war, and colonial competition then weaponized that environment, inventing nearly every disinformation technique still in use. ([Propaganda during the Reformation – Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Propaganda_during_the_Reformation))

## 2. Key Examples

### 2.1 The Donation of Constantine, exposed by Lorenzo Valla (1440)
**Region:** Papal States / Naples. A medieval forgery claiming Emperor Constantine had granted the Pope dominion over the western Roman Empire underpinned papal temporal authority for centuries. Humanist Lorenzo Valla, working under Alfonso of Aragon (then in conflict with Pope Eugene IV), used **philological analysis** to prove the text was a fake — its Latin vocabulary (e.g. *satrap*) and grammar were 8th-century, not 4th. Valla's *De falso credita et ementita Constantini donatione* was suppressed initially but printed by Ulrich von Hutten in 1517 and weaponized by Luther. **Techniques:** debunking; counter-propaganda commission for Alfonso. ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donation_of_Constantine), [History of Information](https://www.historyofinformation.com/detail.php?id=1817))

### 2.2 Simon of Trent Blood Libel (1475+)
**Region:** Trent, Holy Roman Empire. A toddler's body found near a Jewish home triggered a torture-confession trial that destroyed the Jewish community and birthed a martyr cult. The accusation was then **mass-printed** — a "hinge case" where the medieval blood libel went viral. The story appeared in the 1493 *Nuremberg Chronicle* with a woodcut, and Rome added Simon to the Martyrologium Romanum in 1583. **Techniques:** atrocity fabrication, polarization, institutional legitimization via repetition. ([Yale Beinecke: Simon of Trent in Print](https://beinecke.library.yale.edu/article/simon-trent-print), [Berkeley News on Magda Teter's *Blood Libel*](https://news.berkeley.edu/2023/05/05/berkeley-talks-magda-teter-blood-libel/))

### 2.3 The Malleus Maleficarum (1487)
**Region:** Speyer, HRE. Dominican inquisitor Heinrich Kramer wrote the definitive witch-hunting manual after being thrown out of Innsbruck for prosecutorial misconduct. He **forged co-authorship** with the more respected Jakob Sprenger and a fake University of Cologne endorsement to manufacture authority. Printed in ~30 editions before 1670; underwrote 40,000–60,000 executions (85% women). **Techniques:** fake expert endorsement, manufactured authority, moral panic, conspiracy template. ([SFI on the manual's networked diffusion](https://www.santafe.edu/news-center/news/how-a-witch-hunting-manual-and-social-networks-helped-ignite-europes-witch-craze))

### 2.4 Luther's Pamphlet War (1517–1525)
**Region:** Saxony, spreading across Germany. Luther and Lucas Cranach the Elder ran arguably the first **integrated branding-and-propaganda operation**: short vernacular pamphlets, distinctive Wittenberg title-page woodcuts, and savage visual satire (the Pope as Antichrist, monks as devils, the "Papal Ass"). German presses produced **over 7,000 pamphlet editions between 1520 and 1525** — more than double the previous decade. **Techniques:** vernacular accessibility, visual emotion, brand identity, viral replication, polarization. ([LACMA: Luther Goes Viral](https://unframed.lacma.org/2017/02/22/luther-goes-viral))

### 2.5 Spanish Black Legend (1552 onward)
**Region:** Spanish Empire, weaponized in Netherlands & England. Dominican friar Bartolomé de las Casas's *Brevísima relación de la destrucción de las Indias* (1552) — itself an advocacy document that **exaggerated atrocities** to lobby the crown — was translated, re-illustrated (notably by Theodor de Bry's gory engravings, 1598), and reprinted across Protestant Europe as anti-Spanish propaganda for two centuries. **Techniques:** weaponized translation, selective amplification, atrocity imagery, geopolitical framing of a domestic critic. ([Encyclopedia.com on the Black Legend](https://www.encyclopedia.com/humanities/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/black-legend))

### 2.6 French Wars of Religion Pamphlets (1562–1598)
**Region:** France. Catholic League and Huguenots ran parallel pamphlet wars; League polemicists cast Henry III as a biblical "Vilain Herodes" or Ahab to license regicide (Henry was assassinated in 1589). One 1568 Catholic pamphlet exhorted readers to "spill your blood for God, even to the last drop." Engravings glorified and condemned the St. Bartholomew's Day Massacre (1572). **Techniques:** biblical dehumanization, incitement to violence, martyrology, visual atrocity propaganda. ([MDPI: From Ahab to Vilain Herodes](https://www.mdpi.com/2077-1444/14/3/344))

### 2.7 English Civil War Newsbooks (1641–1660)
**Region:** England. Roughly **300 distinct newsbooks** appeared in the 1640s, including the Royalist *Mercurius Aulicus* (printed in Oxford), the Parliamentary *Mercurius Britanicus*, and Bruno Ryves's atrocity-focused *Mercurius Rusticus*. Marchamont Nedham notoriously switched sides three times, inventing the partisan press. **Techniques:** weekly news cadence, paid partisan journalism, satirical persona, side-switching mercenary writers. ([History Hit](https://www.historyhit.com/innovations-in-propaganda-from-the-english-civil-war/))

### 2.8 The Popish Plot (1678–1681)
**Region:** England. Defrocked clergyman Titus Oates fabricated a Jesuit conspiracy to assassinate Charles II. Despite Charles personally catching Oates in lies, the suspicious murder of magistrate Sir Edmund Berry Godfrey supercharged the panic; **hundreds of thousands of pamphlets** flooded London coffeehouses. **22 innocent Catholics were executed.** Oates was later convicted of perjury and pilloried. **Techniques:** false witness, evidence-laundering through a sympathetic martyr (Godfrey), pamphlet saturation, exploitation of latent prejudice. ([Wikipedia: Popish Plot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Popish_Plot))

### 2.9 Boston Massacre Propaganda (1770)
**Region:** Boston, MA. Five colonists died in a chaotic confrontation; Samuel Adams immediately branded it the "Boston Massacre." Paul Revere pirated Henry Pelham's engraving and altered it — showing British soldiers firing on Adams's instruction as a disciplined volley at peaceful citizens, omitting the rocks and ice the crowd had thrown — explicitly to taint the **jury pool** before the soldiers' trial. **Techniques:** branding/naming, image manipulation, jury intimidation, plagiarism of source art. ([History.com](https://www.history.com/news/paul-revere-engraving-boston-massacre))

### 2.10 Franklin's "Supplement to the Boston Independent Chronicle" (1782)
**Region:** Passy, France (Franklin's Paris print shop). Franklin **forged an entire fake issue of a real newspaper**, complete with masthead, ads, and a fabricated report of "Captain Gerrish" describing bales of scalps — including those of women and children — taken by British-allied Native Americans. He fed it to John Adams, John Jay, and trusted couriers for laundering into the British press during peace negotiations. **35 American newspapers reprinted it as fact** before it was debunked in 1854. **Techniques:** fake outlet impersonation, laundered-source amplification, atrocity propaganda for diplomatic leverage. ([Journal of the American Revolution](https://allthingsliberty.com/2014/11/propaganda-warfare-benjamin-franklin-fakes-a-newspaper/))

### 2.11 Le Père Duchesne (1790–1794)
**Region:** Revolutionary Paris. Jacques Hébert wrote 385 issues in the persona of a foul-mouthed working-class pipe-smoker, calling for guillotinings in vernacular slang. The Republic itself printed bulk runs for distribution to the army. Being named in *Père Duchesne* often meant death. **Techniques:** populist persona, voice impersonation, state-funded incitement, character assassination as policy tool. ([Wikipedia: Le Père Duchesne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Le_P%C3%A8re_Duchesne))

## 3. Key Techniques Established

1. **Vernacular mass-pamphleteering** — short, cheap, emotional text in everyday speech (Luther, Paine, Hébert), bypassing scholarly Latin gatekeepers.
2. **Image as argument** — woodcuts and engravings (Cranach, de Bry, Revere) carried polemic to the illiterate and lodged in memory.
3. **Forgery & source impersonation** — fake documents (Donation of Constantine, Malleus's fake endorsements), fake newspapers (Franklin), fake authors. The press made forgery scalable.
4. **Atrocity propaganda** — fabricated or exaggerated violence (blood libels, Black Legend, Franklin scalp report) to dehumanize an enemy.
5. **Partisan periodical journalism** — recurring branded news with persistent editorial line (the *Mercurius* wars, *Père Duchesne*), inventing the partisan press.

## 4. Key Figures for a Codex

- **Lorenzo Valla (1407–1457)** — first modern textual debunker; weaponized philology.
- **Heinrich Kramer (c. 1430–1505)** — inquisitor, forger of authority, author of *Malleus Maleficarum*.
- **Lucas Cranach the Elder (1472–1553)** — visual propagandist who built the Reformation brand.
- **Marchamont Nedham (1620–1678)** — the prototype mercenary partisan journalist; wrote for every side.
- **Titus Oates (1649–1705)** — the perjurer-celebrity whose lies got 22 men hanged.
- **Benjamin Franklin (1706–1790)** — Founding Father and founding forger of fake news.
- **Jacques Hébert (1757–1794)** — populist persona-pamphleteer who fed the Terror.
