Hitler articulated the **große Lüge** doctrine in *Mein Kampf*, Volume 1, Chapter 10 (1925), originally attributing the technique to "the Jews" — and then deploying it against them. His claim: ordinary people, who tell small lies in daily life, find it psychologically harder to disbelieve a colossal lie than a small one, because they cannot imagine its scale. Repeat it without modification, and the falsehood becomes the only available frame.

Joseph Goebbels operationalized this once he was named Reich Minister for Public Enlightenment and Propaganda on **March 13, 1933**. Within weeks the Reichsministerium had absorbed radio, press, theater, film, and the arts under a single ministerial hand. The technical means was the **Volksempfänger** ("people's receiver"), a state-subsidized radio set introduced August 1933 at a deliberately accessible price of 76 Reichsmarks. By 1939 it was in ~70% of German households. Its tuning range was deliberately limited — strong reception of domestic Nazi broadcasts, weak reception of foreign signals.

The combination — doctrinal scale, total media monopoly, and a household-level distribution channel that physically biased toward the regime's voice — produced a propaganda environment qualitatively different from anything democratic states had built. The Soviet apparatus under Stalin reached a similar place by 1937 through *Pravda* and *Izvestiya* monopoly, the Glavlit censorship office, and the airbrushing of purged figures from the photographic record (David King's *The Commissar Vanishes* catalogs hundreds of examples). Two totalitarianisms, one playbook: own the means of utterance, then say anything.
