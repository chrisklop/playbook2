**Denis Sefton Delmer** (1904–1979) was a Berlin-born British journalist for the *Daily Express* who had personally interviewed Hitler in 1933. In May 1941, **Winston Churchill** placed him in charge of the UK's black propaganda broadcasting under the **Political Warfare Executive** (PWE). Black propaganda is defined by its lying source: where *white* propaganda openly declares its origin (the BBC announcing it's the BBC), and *grey* propaganda is unattributed, **black propaganda claims to be coming from inside the enemy population itself**.

Delmer's first station, **Gustav Siegfried Eins ("GS1")**, launched May 23, 1941. Its presenter, "Der Chef," was scripted as a disgruntled patriotic Wehrmacht officer who hated the British but hated Nazi Party corruption more. The on-air persona fabricated sex scandals about senior Nazi officials, attacked SS profiteering, and "leaked" plausible-sounding rumors of dissent within the High Command. Listeners had to assume it was being broadcast clandestinely from within Germany — when in fact every transmission came from a country house in **Aspley Guise, Bedfordshire**.

In 1943 Delmer launched **Soldatensender Calais** ("Soldiers' Station Calais"), aimed at Wehrmacht troops on the Western Front. Soldatensender used the **Aspidistra transmitter** — at 600 kilowatts the most powerful broadcaster in Europe, originally bought from RCA — to overpower German stations and pose as a real military broadcast. The station played the latest American swing recordings (often weeks before they could be heard on German civilian radio), gave accurate weather forecasts, and read genuine personal greetings from real German POWs whose voices the British had recorded. Embedded in that credibility was a steady drip of demoralizing rumor: leadership infighting, manufactured shortages, invented disasters on other fronts.

Post-war polling and surrendered-soldier interviews suggested **~40% of German troops in the West listened to Soldatensender Calais regularly**, many believing it was a real Wehrmacht station. The technique — a real-broadcast-with-fake-attribution that mixed verifiable truth with calibrated lies — is the direct ancestor of every modern influence-operation impersonation. It works because the *form* (a familiar voice, the right music, accurate weather) authenticates the *content* (the lies).

Delmer's memoir *Black Boomerang* (1962) is the standard primary source. He believed the technique should be retired with the war that justified it; he was not entirely surprised when, by the 1950s, both blocs of the Cold War were running their own.
