The official UK report on alleged German atrocities in occupied Belgium, chaired by Viscount James Bryce (former ambassador to the United States), was released **May 12, 1915**. Translated into every major European language, it was a primary instrument in turning American opinion toward intervention.

Its method became the template for industrial-age atrocity propaganda: pair real, documented crimes with sensationalized invented details. Modern scholarship (John Horne and Alan Kramer, *German Atrocities, 1914*) confirms that several thousand Belgian civilians **were** executed by the German army during the 1914 invasion — collective reprisals against suspected francs-tireurs. But Appendix A of the Bryce Report layered onto these real events lurid accounts of mutilated babies, systematic public rape, and bayoneted children that subsequent investigation could not corroborate.

The technique's dark legacy outlasted its purpose. When Allied intelligence received the first reliable reports of the Nazi extermination camps in late 1942, **Victor Cavendish-Bentinck**, chair of the Joint Intelligence Committee, dismissed them as a recycled "corpse-factory lie" — a direct reference to the German Corpse Factory hoax that British black propaganda had invented in 1917 and that Brigadier-General John Charteris later admitted fabricating. Atrocity calibration created the immune response to real atrocity. The lie poisoned the well for the truth that followed it.
