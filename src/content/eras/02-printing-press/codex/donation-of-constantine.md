A medieval forgery claiming Emperor Constantine had granted the Pope dominion over the western Roman Empire underpinned papal temporal authority for centuries. The document was treated as foundational law — cited in disputes between popes and kings, embedded in canon law collections, and used to justify the Papal States themselves.

Working under Alfonso of Aragon (then in conflict with Pope Eugene IV), humanist Lorenzo Valla used philological analysis to prove the text was a fake. Its Latin vocabulary — including anachronistic terms like *satrap* — and grammar were 8th-century, not 4th. Valla's *De falso credita et ementita Constantini donatione* invented the modern textual debunk.

Suppressed at first, the treatise was printed by Ulrich von Hutten in 1517 and weaponized by Luther as evidence that Rome's entire claim to temporal power rested on a manufactured document. Forgery scaled by the press could be exposed only by counter-forensics scaled by the same press.
