Dominican inquisitor Heinrich Kramer wrote the definitive witch-hunting manual after being expelled from Innsbruck for prosecutorial misconduct — a local bishop had thrown out his case, citing his fixation on the female defendants' sexual lives. The *Malleus Maleficarum* was his comeback.

To manufacture authority, Kramer forged co-authorship with the more respected Dominican Jakob Sprenger and fabricated an endorsement from the University of Cologne's theology faculty. Modern scholarship treats Sprenger's involvement as minimal at best, and the Cologne approbation as substantially doctored.

The book ran through roughly thirty editions before 1670 and traveled on the press's new viral logistics. Networked diffusion turned a single inquisitor's grievance into the operating manual for a continent-spanning witch craze, underwriting an estimated 40,000–60,000 executions, roughly 85% of them women. Fake expert endorsement, manufactured authority, and moral panic — all of it became a reusable conspiracy template.
