In **President Bush's State of the Union address on January 28, 2003** — eight weeks before the U.S. invasion of Iraq — he delivered the sentence that would become known as the "sixteen words":

> *"The British government has learned that Saddam Hussein recently sought significant quantities of uranium from Africa."*

The "African country" was Niger. The "uranium" was yellowcake — a milled uranium concentrate used as feedstock for enrichment.

The underlying claim came from a set of documents purporting to be Nigerien diplomatic correspondence describing a uranium-export agreement with Iraq. The documents had been peddled around Western intelligence services since October 2002. **By March 2003, the IAEA had determined the documents were crude forgeries** — referring to officials no longer in office, formatted incorrectly, and containing internal inconsistencies an investigator could catch in an afternoon. The U.S. National Intelligence Council had warned the White House the claim was unreliable in October 2002, before the speech was drafted.

The mechanism that produced the sixteen-words sentence:

- **CIA director George Tenet** had personally requested the line be removed from an October 2002 Cincinnati speech. The line was removed.
- **It returned, sourced to British intelligence** for the State of the Union three months later. (The British government later defended the claim on the basis of *other* intelligence; that intelligence has never been publicly produced.)
- **Joseph Wilson** — a former U.S. ambassador the CIA had sent to Niger in early 2002 to investigate the claim — wrote a New York Times op-ed in July 2003 saying he had found no evidence of any such transaction.
- **Within a week of Wilson's op-ed**, his wife **Valerie Plame** — a covert CIA officer — was publicly identified in a Robert Novak column. The leak originated in the Office of the Vice President and led to the only U.S. criminal conviction of the entire WMD intelligence cycle (Scooter Libby, perjury).

What this case demonstrates is **the structural laundering pattern** that defines the WMD episode generally:

1. **An unreliable foreign source produces a document.**
2. **Allied intelligence services accept it under a separate provenance** ("a friendly service tells us…"), removing the original's authorship from the chain.
3. **The repackaged claim enters a major speech**, where its sourcing is now untraceable to the public.
4. **By the time the document itself is exposed as a forgery**, the policy decisions justified by it are irreversible.

The Niger forgeries are one of three core pillars of pre-war Iraqi WMD claims that have since been completely retracted (the others being the **CURVEBALL** mobile-bioweapon-lab claim and the **aluminum-tube centrifuge** misclassification). The Iraq war proceeded.
