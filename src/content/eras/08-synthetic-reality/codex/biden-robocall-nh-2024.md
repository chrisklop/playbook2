Two days before the **January 23, 2024** New Hampshire primary, an estimated **5,000 to 25,000 Democratic households** received a robocall in what sounded exactly like President Joe Biden's voice, telling them not to vote in the primary and to "save your vote for the November election." Voting in the primary, of course, did not preclude voting in November.

The audio was generated by a tool from **ElevenLabs**. The political consultant who commissioned it, **Steve Kramer**, later said he paid roughly **$150** to a New Orleans street magician for the work. A second analysis of the underlying tooling suggested the actual voice-clone generation step cost less than **$1**.

The FCC unanimously declared AI-generated robocalls illegal under the existing Telephone Consumer Protection Act on **February 8, 2024** — sixteen days after the incident. The agency had not previously needed to address whether a "voice" had to be human.

Mechanically, this is a clean demonstration of the new economics of disinformation:

- **The marginal cost of fabricating a public figure's voice is approaching zero.** Earlier eras required actors, recording studios, and editing. The 2024 stack required eleven seconds of source audio and a $1 inference call.
- **The legal and detection infrastructure trails the capability by months to years.** Federal rulemaking against AI robocalls existed within weeks of this incident — fast by regulatory standards, but the next election cycle is already different.
- **The political effect does not require the deepfake to be believed.** Even after the call was widely reported as fake, it had achieved its core purpose: planting doubt about whether to vote, in a primary where Biden was not on the ballot.

This was the first synthetic-voice attack on a U.S. presidential primary. It will not be the last.
