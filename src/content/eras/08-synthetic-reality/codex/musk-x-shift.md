**Elon Musk closed the acquisition of Twitter on October 27, 2022**, paying **$44 billion** — about $13.5B of his own equity, the rest borrowed against the company. He fired CEO Parag Agrawal, CFO Ned Segal, and chief legal officer Vijaya Gadde in the first 24 hours, and laid off approximately **half of Twitter's ~7,500 employees** within two weeks.

The product changes that followed reshaped the largest text-based public-discourse platform in the world:

- **Verification became paid**. Blue checkmarks had previously marked accounts of public interest the company had verified. Starting November 2022, anyone could buy one for **$8/month** ("Twitter Blue," later "X Premium"). The signal collapsed within weeks — impersonators bought checkmarks to pose as Eli Lilly (causing a real stock drop), Lockheed Martin, the Pope, and senators.
- **Trust & Safety was dismantled**. The Trust & Safety Council was disbanded December 2022. Yoel Roth, head of Trust & Safety, resigned. Content moderation staffing was reduced by roughly 80% globally.
- **Suspended accounts were reinstated.** Including Donald Trump, Kanye West, Andrew Tate, Alex Jones, Roger Stone, and Marjorie Taylor Greene's personal account.
- **The algorithm was reweighted.** Internal documents leaked in mid-2023 showed paid accounts receiving roughly 4× the impressions of unpaid accounts on identical posts. The "For You" feed was reweighted to favor engagement (defined to include outrage replies and quote-tweets).
- **The API was paywalled.** Free research access ended in February 2023. The "Enterprise" tier costs roughly **$42,000 per month**, ending most academic study of platform-level disinformation in a single move.

Musk rebranded the company to **X** in July 2023. By late 2024, daily active users were reported flat-to-down versus pre-acquisition; advertiser revenue had fallen roughly 50%. The product is structurally different from the platform that existed before — and the change happened fast enough that comparative academic work mostly cannot be done.

This is the cleanest live example of what it means when a major distribution layer for public discourse is owned by a single individual with discretionary content policy and no inherited institutional resistance. The Hearst-and-Pulitzer playbook of Era 3 required decades of newspaper capital to acquire. The X acquisition required a Friday afternoon.
