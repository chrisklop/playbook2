On **April 18, 2023**, **Fox News paid Dominion Voting Systems $787.5 million** to settle a defamation lawsuit moments before jury selection was to begin in Wilmington, Delaware. It is the largest publicly-disclosed media defamation settlement in U.S. history — roughly **23% of Fox Corporation's annual operating income** at the time.

Dominion's complaint, filed March 2021, was that Fox News had knowingly broadcast false claims that Dominion's voting machines had switched votes from Trump to Biden in the 2020 election. To prevail, Dominion had to clear the **"actual malice"** standard from *New York Times v. Sullivan* (1964): proof that the network either *knew* the claims were false or acted with *reckless disregard* for whether they were true.

Discovery produced the documents that made the settlement inevitable. Internal Fox communications surfaced in the case showed:

- **Tucker Carlson**, to a producer: *"I hate him passionately"* (re: Trump). *"What he's good at is destroying things. He could easily destroy us if we play it wrong."*
- **Sean Hannity**, in deposition: *"I did not believe it for one second"* (re: Dominion vote-flipping claims).
- **Rupert Murdoch**, in deposition: agreed that Fox hosts had *"endorsed"* the false election narrative, and that he could have stopped them but did not.
- An on-air host privately calling Sidney Powell's claims **"absolutely insane."**
- A producer noting that real journalism would have **"impacted the [stock] price negatively"** because Fox's audience was migrating to Newsmax over insufficiently-pro-Trump coverage.

The settlement was a financial penalty but not a corporate-behavior change. Fox issued a 30-second on-air acknowledgment that the court had found "certain claims about Dominion to be false" — no apology, no retraction by name, no anchor termination tied to the case. (Carlson was fired a week later on unrelated grounds.)

The lasting public-interest contribution of the case is the **discovery record itself**. It is the most extensive evidentiary corpus the U.S. legal system has produced documenting that a major cable news network can knowingly broadcast claims its own staff considered false, in pursuit of audience retention. The depositions and emails are publicly archived; they will be referenced by media scholars for decades.
