**NewsGuard**, a service that rates the credibility of news websites for ad-tech buyers, maintains an **Unreliable AI-Generated News Sites (UAINS)** tracker. The published count in **May 2023** was 49. By mid-2024 it had passed **1,200**. The sites share an operational fingerprint: domains that mimic real publications (`countycitytribune.com`, `boston-tribune.online`), generic mastheads, no listed editors, no contact information beyond a Gmail address, and articles whose internal structure shows the unmistakable cadence of an LLM with the wrappers stripped off.

A 2023 NYT investigation traced one site's "About Us" page to text directly generated by ChatGPT — including the placeholder phrase *"As an AI language model, I cannot…"* left accidentally in the published copy.

The economics are simple. The sites monetize through **programmatic advertising** — automated ad auctions in which real brands' ads are placed onto pages they have never reviewed. Google's AdSense, the largest such network, has been documented placing major-brand ads on UAINS-tracked sites. The advertiser pays; the operator collects; the brand has no idea where the impressions came from.

The scale at which this is now possible is qualitatively new:

- **An LLM produces 200 articles per day per machine** at a marginal cost approaching free.
- **Domain registration is $9.99**, and domain-similarity to a real outlet is unprotected absent trademark action.
- **The detection problem is asymmetric**: identifying one synthetic site requires manual review; producing one requires zero.

These are not viral or high-traffic sites individually. Most receive a few thousand visits per month. What they do collectively is **launder citations** — a politician's claim, or a foreign-influence narrative, can be "supported" by a dozen sources that look like local newspapers, and that turn up in Google searches above the genuine local press because the genuine local press is dying.
