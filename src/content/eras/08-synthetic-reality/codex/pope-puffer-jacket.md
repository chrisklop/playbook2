On **March 24, 2023**, an anonymous Reddit user posted four images of **Pope Francis** wearing an oversized white **Balenciaga** puffer coat. The images were generated using **Midjourney v5**, which had been released to the public ten days earlier.

The pictures spread through Twitter, Instagram, and TikTok over the following 72 hours. Estimated reach: **20+ million impressions** before any major outlet flagged the images as synthetic. Notable believers included a national-level radio host, multiple verified accounts, and — by the photographer Chrissy Teigen's own admission — most of her followers.

What makes this case historically important is not that it fooled people. Earlier hoaxes have fooled people for centuries. What is new is the **production cost structure**:

- **The image cost approximately 20 cents and 45 seconds** to generate via Midjourney's subscription pricing.
- **No specialized skill was required.** The prompt was reportedly something like *"Pope Francis, white Balenciaga puffer coat, candid Vatican paparazzi shot."*
- **The original poster has never been identified** and faced no consequences.
- **No formal correction process exists.** Wikipedia eventually added a paragraph; Reuters and AP wrote explainers. The images themselves continued to circulate.

Three further patterns this incident established:

1. **The puffer coat itself** — a recognizable fashion item, expensive but contemporary — produced a stronger sense of plausibility than a more obvious anachronism would have. Synthetic media succeeds when its surface details match the texture of the period.
2. **The "candid" framing** (cropped, slightly blurred, mid-stride) is now the most reliable visual signature of an AI image trying to look real. Subsequent deepfakes deliberately reproduce this aesthetic.
3. **Believer cohorts skewed older** in early surveys. The puffer-pope is the first AI image many people over 50 saw without realising it; the impression that "you can't trust photos anymore" entered the demographic that votes most reliably.

Less than one year separates this image from the first U.S. presidential primary deepfake. The infrastructure for synthetic public figures was complete by spring 2023; the political application followed nine months later.
