**White evangelical Protestants comprise approximately 14% of U.S. adults** (Pew, 2020s estimates) but **have delivered 76–81% of their presidential vote to the Republican candidate in every cycle since 1980** — the most reliable demographic voting bloc in modern American politics by margin.

**Specific to the 2016 cycle**: Donald Trump received approximately **81% of white evangelical Protestant votes** per network exit polling — the **highest share of the white evangelical vote for any candidate in the exit-polling era**. (For context: George W. Bush in 2004, also a self-identified evangelical, received ~78%.) Trump's coalition with the bloc held: he received ~76% in 2020 and ~81% in 2024.

The historical paradox the social-science literature has been working through since 2016: Trump's personal biography (three marriages, documented infidelities, allegations of sexual misconduct from ~25 women, no record of public religious practice) is unlike any prior evangelical candidate. The bloc nevertheless cohered around him faster and more durably than around George W. Bush, who was the most overtly religiously expressive U.S. president of the modern era.

The mechanisms documented in religion-and-politics research:

1. **Long-built infrastructure**. The voter-mobilization apparatus assembled by the Moral Majority (Falwell, 1979–1989), the Christian Coalition (Robertson/Reed, 1989–1997, see Era 5 codex), the Family Research Council, and Liberty Counsel was operating at full scale by 2016 — entirely independently of any specific candidate's biography.

2. **Judicial appointments as the contract**. The deal Trump made explicit (in May 2016, during the candidate phase) was that **his Supreme Court picks would come from a Federalist Society / Heritage Foundation–approved list**. The 6–3 conservative majority on the Roberts Court (Gorsuch 2017, Kavanaugh 2018, Barrett 2020) was the contract delivered. *Dobbs v. Jackson Women's Health* (2022) ratified it.

3. **Pastoral endorsements as legitimacy laundering**. **Robert Jeffress** (First Baptist Dallas), **Paula White** (presidential-spiritual-advisor), **Franklin Graham** (Samaritan's Purse), **Jerry Falwell Jr.** (Liberty University), and others provided the public theological framing under which evangelical voters could endorse a candidate whose personal life conflicted with stated evangelical morality. The construction: King Cyrus of Persia — God's chosen instrument despite being a pagan.

4. **The "fighter" framing**. Survey research from PRRI and Pew through 2017–2020 documented that white evangelicals reported the strongest agreement (versus other religious groups) with statements like *"the country has changed in ways I don't recognize"* and *"a strong leader is needed even if some democratic norms must be set aside."* This framing predates Trump in the bloc; he was the candidate who matched it.

5. **The megachurch broadcast layer**. Live-stream Sunday services from Lakewood (Joel Osteen), Saddleback, Crossroads, and similar megachurches reach combined weekly audiences in the millions. Most do not endorse candidates from the pulpit (501(c)(3) constraints); but the worldview, vocabulary, and topical framing of their broadcasts overlap heavily with the Republican Party's framing of contemporary cultural issues.
