The **Internet Research Agency (IRA)** was a Russian commercial entity headquartered in **St. Petersburg**, operating from a four-story building at **55 Savushkina Street** through the early-to-mid 2010s. It was funded by **Yevgeny Prigozhin** — the same Prigozhin who later founded the Wagner Group mercenary network and, in 2023, staged a brief mutiny against the Russian state before dying in a plane crash.

**The Mueller Report (Volume I, March 2019)** documented IRA's U.S.-targeted political operation in detail. Key findings:

- **Operations began at least in 2014**, targeting Black American social-media users on issues of police violence, then expanded to a broader spectrum of U.S. political identity groups by 2015–2016.
- **The IRA created and operated thousands of social-media accounts** posing as American activists across the political spectrum. Examples: *@TEN_GOP* (claiming to be Tennessee Republican Party — over 100K followers), *Blacktivist* (Black Lives Matter–aligned), *United Muslims of America* (anti-Trump Muslim activist), *Heart of Texas* (secessionist).
- **Facebook estimated IRA content reached approximately 126 million U.S. accounts** during the relevant period. Roughly 30 million accounts shared IRA content with their own networks; the resharing chain produced exponentially larger reach.
- **The IRA organized real-world rallies on U.S. soil** — including, on at least one documented occasion, **two competing rallies in the same city on the same day**, both funded and promoted by the IRA, designed to produce physical confrontation between American citizens.
- **The IRA's operational budget for U.S. operations averaged ~$1.25 million per month** by 2016. Per-impression cost was below market rate by an order of magnitude.

The Mueller Report indicted 13 IRA personnel and three IRA-related entities in February 2018. None faced trial; all were in Russia and outside U.S. extradition reach.

Two things make this the canonical case study of industrial-scale state-sponsored disinformation:

1. **The operation was profit-structured.** Prigozhin paid operatives roughly $700–1,000/month (per Russian press accounts) in St. Petersburg market wages. The product wasn't ideological purity; it was engagement metrics, contracted out at a cheaper unit cost than any Western alternative.
2. **The targets were U.S. domestic divisions**, not Russian foreign-policy objectives. The IRA's Black Lives Matter–aligned content was as carefully produced as its pro-Trump content. The goal was internal U.S. friction; either side providing more friction was equally valuable.

After 2017, the IRA's known operations contracted under U.S. sanctions and Facebook account purges. Functional equivalents — sometimes operated by the same individuals through new corporate shells — have been documented through 2023 (in Ukraine, in African elections, and around Western policy debates). The model is portable. The 2016 operation is the most-documented version of it.
