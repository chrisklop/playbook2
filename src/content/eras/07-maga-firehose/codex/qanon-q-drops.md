**QAnon** began on **October 28, 2017**, when an anonymous user identifying as **"Q Clearance Patriot"** posted on **4chan's /pol/ board** claiming to be a senior U.S. military intelligence official with access to classified information about a covert war between President Trump and a satanic cabal of pedophile elites within the U.S. government, Democratic Party, and Hollywood.

Over the next **three years and two months**, the same identity posted approximately **4,953 messages** (called **"drops"** or **"crumbs"** by adherents), migrating from 4chan to 8chan to 8kun as platforms restructured around the content. The drops were typically:

- **Cryptic**: a few words, an enigmatic question, or a screenshot with circled portions
- **Predictive**: specific events that, by a vague timeline, were supposed to come to pass
- **Unfalsifiable**: when predicted events did not occur, "the Plan" had simply not been ready

The **community's role was active interpretation**. Volunteer "bread bakers" — a term derived from the 4chan culture of posting "bread" (threads) — produced thousands of pages of analysis, video walkthroughs, and YouTube live-streams decoding each new drop. The mechanism is structurally important: the audience did the work of producing the conspiracy narrative, then defended it as their own intellectual product.

**Forensic linguistic analysis published by Bellingcat in April 2021**, combining stylometry, posting-time patterns, and metadata from leaked moderator screenshots, attributed the Q identity to **Jim Watkins and his son Ron Watkins** — the operators of 8chan/8kun. Ron Watkins served as the platform's administrator and had unrestricted technical access to posting timestamps; multiple analysts independently traced the Q account's continuity through Watkins's known travel patterns.

The doctrinal core of QAnon — **"Where We Go One, We Go All"** (WWG1WGA), the imminent "Storm" of mass arrests, the John F. Kennedy Jr. faked-death return, the secret Trump-as-savior framing — produced one of the most durable online conspiracy communities in U.S. history. PRRI polling in 2021 found:

- **15% of Americans agreed** with the core QAnon belief that the government, media, and finance are controlled by a satanic cabal.
- **20% agreed** with the claim that "true American patriots may have to resort to violence in order to save our country."
- The overlap was non-trivial: roughly 25 million U.S. adults held at least one core QAnon belief.

By the **January 6, 2021 Capitol breach**, QAnon imagery and rhetoric were visibly present in the crowd. The widely-photographed **Jacob Chansley** ("QAnon Shaman") wore facepaint, fur, and a horned headdress for the news cycles that followed; he served just over three years of a 41-month sentence.

The structural innovation worth tracking: QAnon was the first major U.S. conspiracy movement built **without a centralized broadcaster**. There was no Glenn Beck giving the audience a chalkboard to follow. The audience was the broadcaster. The platform owner (Watkins) merely seeded the prompts. This is the operational ancestor of every distributed-narrative attack to follow — including, by 2023, the LLM-amplified versions of the same play.
