In the months leading up to November 10, 1898, North Carolina's white-supremacist Democrats — coordinating with Josephus Daniels's *News and Observer* and the *Wilmington Messenger* — flooded the state with fabricated and inflammatory stories alleging that Black men were threatening white women. A doctored, deliberately misrepresented editorial by Black newspaper editor Alex Manly was reprinted across the region as proof.

The campaign manufactured the pretext for a violent coup that overthrew Wilmington's legitimately elected biracial municipal government. White mobs killed dozens of Black citizens — exact counts remain disputed — burned Manly's *Daily Record* offices, and forced surviving Black officeholders out of town at gunpoint.

It is the only successful coup d'état on American soil, and it was engineered by newspapers. The case shows how coordinated multi-newspaper "rape-scare" campaigns, combined with selective quotation, can build the consent and the mob necessary to physically overthrow a democratic election. It also marks the start of a half-century of de facto disenfranchisement in North Carolina.
