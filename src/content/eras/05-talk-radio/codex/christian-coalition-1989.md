Pat Robertson founded the **Christian Coalition of America** in 1989, immediately after his unsuccessful 1988 Republican presidential primary run. The mailing list from that campaign — roughly 1.8 million names — became the founding subscriber base. The 28-year-old **Ralph Reed**, plucked from a Republican Party operative role in Georgia, ran the organization day-to-day until 1997.

The signature tactic was the **voter guide**: a one-page leaflet comparing candidates on a fixed set of issues (abortion, school prayer, tax cuts, gun rights), arranged so that the Republican candidate's positions were consistently presented as the "biblical" ones.

- The guides did not say "vote for X" — that would have voided the organization's 501(c)(4) tax-exempt status.
- They were distributed in church bulletins, narthex stacks, and pew literature on the Sunday before Election Tuesday.
- **33 million guides were distributed for the 1992 election.** **45 million** for 1996.

The 1996 IRS investigation found the practice insufficiently nonpartisan and stripped the organization of its tax exemption — but by then the model had been institutionalized across thousands of churches independent of the Coalition itself.

The broader pattern: a religious civic identity was wired into a partisan-vote-mobilization machine, with the church newsletter as last-mile delivery. The infrastructure persisted long after the Coalition fragmented; the **white evangelical voting bloc** that delivered roughly 80% of its votes to Republican presidential candidates from 2000 onward was, in substantial part, the constituency Robertson and Reed had organized.
