Limbaugh's syndicated program launched **August 1, 1988** out of WABC New York — twelve months after the FCC repealed the Fairness Doctrine. He had spent the previous four years building the format at KFBK in Sacramento, where he replaced Morton Downey Jr. and discovered that a one-host monologue, with callers used as a chorus rather than as guests with opposing views, produced more loyal audiences than any conventional talk format.

The mechanics were deliberate:

- **Three-hour daily block**, weekdays, never broken.
- **Callers as affirmation**, not debate. "Dittos, Rush" became the in-group greeting — agreement so reflexive it stopped being argued.
- **No on-air apology, no correction segments**, by editorial design.
- A signature device he called the **"EIB Network"** ("Excellence in Broadcasting") — a fictional, single-host "network" that gave the syndication a brand identity larger than any one station.

By 1993, the show ran on **650+ AM stations** with a weekly audience estimated at **20 million**. By 2008, *Talkers Magazine* placed weekly cume above 14 million; even after declines, it remained the most-listened-to talk radio program in the country until Limbaugh's death in 2021.

The political consequence: a parallel media organ that could set the GOP base's agenda independent of campaign committees or the party press operation. Bob Dole called Limbaugh the "voice of America." House Republicans gave him an honorary membership of their 1994 freshman class.
