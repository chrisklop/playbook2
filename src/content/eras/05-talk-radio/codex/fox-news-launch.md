**Fox News Channel launched October 7, 1996** in 17 million U.S. cable households. Rupert Murdoch put up roughly **$900 million** in startup capital, including ~$11/subscriber in carriage-fee inducements to cable system operators — an inversion of the normal market in which networks pay for distribution. The decisive hire was **Roger Ailes**, who left CNBC after a fight over a planned business channel.

Ailes had been here before. In **1970**, as a 30-year-old Nixon White House media consultant, he drafted a memo titled *"A Plan for Putting the GOP on TV News"* — proposing a national television service to bypass the "filter" of the existing network news. The memo sat in Republican circles for a quarter century. The cable rollout finally provided the distribution layer that made the original plan economically viable.

Operating choices that defined the channel:

- **"Fair and Balanced"** as a brand promise, paired with a panel format that built false-balance into the room composition itself (one credentialed expert; one operative).
- The **lower-third chyron** as a separate editorial channel — the on-screen text often advanced a narrative the anchor's spoken script did not.
- A **morning–evening pipeline** in which prime-time hosts reacted to *Fox & Friends* segments, which had reacted to Drudge, which had reacted to talk radio.
- Within six years (2002), Fox News passed CNN to become the most-watched cable news network in the U.S. — a position it has not relinquished.

The economic mechanism is worth flagging: **carriage fees** mean every U.S. cable subscriber pays Fox News whether they watch or not. The network's revenue floor doesn't depend on tuning in. That's a structurally different business than ad-supported television — and structurally different from the kind of audience accountability the pre-cable broadcast era enforced.
